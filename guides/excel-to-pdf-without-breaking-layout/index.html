How to convert Excel to PDF without breaking the layout

Spreadsheets are built for screens, not paper. When you convert one to PDF, wide tables can be cut off, tiny text becomes unreadable, and numbers can spill onto extra pages. A few minutes of preparation prevents most of these problems.

Check the sheet before converting

  • Delete empty rows and columns at the edges so the table is as compact as possible.
  • Make sure columns are wide enough that numbers do not show as ####.
  • Put a clear header in the first row of each sheet.
  • Remove hidden notes or private columns you do not want to share.

Choose landscape for wide tables

If your sheet has more than about eight columns, use landscape pages. This gives each column more room and keeps the text at a readable size. Portrait works well for narrow lists such as price lists or contact sheets.

Know what is kept

The converter shows the values exactly as displayed in Excel, including dates and currency. Formulas are not carried over, only their results. Colours, merged cells and charts are simplified, so check these if they matter.

Large workbooks

Every sheet becomes its own section. If your file has many sheets or thousands of rows, the PDF can become long. Consider splitting the file, or keeping only the sheets you need.

Before you send it

Scroll through the PDF, check the last page, and look for clipped text. If you need to share data that others will edit, send the original Excel file instead and use the PDF only for reading or printing.
